# Objection — "Just give me the estimate by phone or email"

## The response {#response}

> "Happy to get you that number — and the good news is the estimate is completely free. The only thing is, a real replacement quote can't be done over the phone. Two identical houses can need very different systems depending on the layout, the ductwork, the insulation, and what's already there. If I give you a number sight-unseen, it's a guess — and a guess almost always costs you more, either because it's padded to cover unknowns or because it balloons once a crew shows up. Our comfort specialist comes out, measures everything, and gives you a written price with Good/Better/Best options that's guaranteed in writing — the number we quote is the number you pay. And right now we're running an exciting promo with 0% interest financing for 25 months, so once you have that written quote we can also show you what the investment looks like as a low monthly payment. It takes about an hour, it costs you nothing, and you have zero obligation. I can have someone there tomorrow — does morning or afternoon work better?"

## Why the customer is saying this {#why-saying-it}

Remote-estimate requests happen rarely — roughly 2% of estimate calls — but they're usually from serious buyers, not tire-kickers. Identify the real driver:

| What they say | What they usually mean | What to answer |
|---|---|---|
| "Just email me a quote." | They're collecting numbers to compare, and a site visit feels like a big commitment. | A written quote we stand behind beats a number we revise later. Free, one hour, no obligation. |
| "How much is a new system, roughly?" | They want to know if they can afford it before investing time. | Range honestly, then anchor to the free visit: "Most homes fall between X and Y depending on the home — the free visit tells you exactly where yours lands. And we're currently offering 0% interest financing for 25 months, so we can also walk you through monthly options once you have the written quote." |
| "The other company gave me a price over the phone." | They think remote quotes are normal. | Explain what a phone quote really is: padded or a bait number. Our written quote is the number you actually pay. |
| "I'm too busy for an appointment." | Scheduling friction, not refusal. | Compress the commitment: "About an hour, we work around your schedule — early morning and evening slots available." |
| "I already have a written quote from someone else." | They want a comparison number without another visit. | Offer a second-opinion visit — free — where we review their quote and measure properly. Their quote becomes our baseline. |
| "Just send me pricing for a 3-ton system." | They've done research and think equipment size = price. | Size is only half the price. Installation conditions drive the rest — that's what the free visit measures. |
| "I'm a landlord, I just want the cheapest option." | Cost-driven, wants minimal involvement. | Even more reason to measure — the cheapest wrong install becomes their recurring emergency call. One free visit, three written options. |

These callers are close to buying — they've already decided to replace. The only sale left is the visit itself.

## Why we don't estimate replacements remotely {#why}

A replacement price depends on things that cannot be seen or heard over the phone:

- **The home itself** — square footage, layout, insulation, windows, orientation. This is what the load calculation measures, and it determines what size system the home actually needs.
- **The existing installation** — ductwork condition, line sets, electrical capacity, access, code items. Two identical units can be thousands apart because of what's around them.
- **What the customer actually wants** — comfort problems, air quality, noise, budget. That conversation happens in the home, looking at the equipment together.

A number given without these is one of two things: **padded** to protect the company against unknowns, or **low** to win the job and revised later. Neither is fair to the customer. Our written on-site quote is a number we guarantee — that's the whole point of measuring first.

## The handling ladder {#ladder}

1. **Lead with free:** "The estimate is completely free — there's no cost and no obligation."
2. **Agree with the goal:** "You want a real number you can plan around — exactly right."
3. **Explain why remote fails:** "Two identical houses can need very different systems. A phone number is a guess, and guesses get padded or revised."
4. **Sell the guarantee:** "We measure everything and give you a written price with Good/Better/Best options. The number we quote is the number you pay."
5. **Shrink the time:** "It takes about an hour, and we work around your schedule."
6. **Close on a choice:** "Does morning or afternoon work better?"

## Scenario responses {#scenarios}

**"Just give me a ballpark."**

> "I can give you a general range based on the size of your home and the type of system — but I want to be honest with you that it's a wide range until someone measures the home, because installation conditions move the number a lot. Where your home lands in that range is exactly what the free visit tells you, in writing, with Good/Better/Best options. About an hour, zero obligation. What day works for you?"

**"Another company quoted me over the phone."**

> "Then you already have a number to compare — but here's the question worth asking them: is that the number you'll actually pay, or the number that gets revised once they see the job? Our quote is different: we measure first, then give you a written price that's guaranteed. Free visit, written number, no surprises."

**"I'm too busy for an appointment."**

> "I completely understand — that's exactly why we keep it to about an hour and schedule around you, including early morning and evening windows. It's the one hour that gets you a number you can actually plan around. What's the least painful day this week?"

**"Email me the quote and I'll think about it."**

> "I'd love to send you something, but anything I email without seeing the home would be a guess — and I don't want to give you a number we can't stand behind. Here's what I can do: our specialist comes out, measures everything, and you get the written quote in hand the same visit, with no obligation. If the number doesn't work, you've lost nothing but an hour. Morning or afternoon?"

**Landlord: "I just want the cheapest system for a rental."**

> "That makes sense — and for a rental, the cheapest *right* system matters more than the cheapest system. An undersized or poorly matched unit becomes your recurring emergency call, and tenants don't wait. One free visit gets you a written price on a system that won't generate callbacks — with options at different price points. When can our specialist get access?"

### The truly adamant caller {#adamant}

If they still refuse the visit after the full ladder, don't argue and don't let the lead die:

1. **Take their email and send a starting-range overview** with Good/Better/Best context — clearly labeled as a range, not a quote.
2. **Book a follow-up call** before you hang up: "I'll send that over now, and I'll call you Thursday to walk through it — if you're ready for an exact written number by then, we'll get a specialist out."
3. **Log it as a nurture lead** and enter the follow-up cadence — an adamant remote-estimate caller who gets a professional range plus a scheduled follow-up books the visit within two weeks more often than not.

Never pretend the range is a quote, and never let "email me something" end the relationship.

## Hard rules {#rules}

- **Never email or phone a specific replacement price.** Ranges are allowed, clearly labeled as ranges.
- **Always lead with free.** The visit costs nothing — most remote-quote requests die the moment they hear that.
- **Never criticize the competitor who gave a phone quote.** Question the number, not the company.
- **Never let the call end without a next step** — a booked visit, or a booked follow-up call.
- **If they already have a written quote,** pivot to the free second-opinion visit rather than re-arguing the process.

## Scoring {#scoring}

- **0** = quoted a specific price remotely, criticized a competitor, or let the call end with no next step.
- **1** = held the process but let the lead go without a booking or a scheduled follow-up.
- **2** = explained why measurement matters, led with free, and booked the visit or a firm follow-up call.
