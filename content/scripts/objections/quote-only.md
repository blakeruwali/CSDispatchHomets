# Objection — "I just want a quote"

## The response {#response}

> "I hear you — you want a real number, not a guess. That's why we follow our Home+ Protocol. Step 2 is 'We Diagnose On-Site' — we LiDAR-scan your home, run a Manual J load calculation, and test airflow, static pressure, refrigerant charge, electrical, and combustion safety. The {{price:diagnostic_residential}} covers that full 60–90 minute visit, your written diagnostic report with photos and 3D scan visuals, and a Good / Better / Best quote signed before any work begins. If you approve the repair today, that diagnostic fee is credited toward the work."

## First, make sure it's actually an objection {#clarify}

"I just want a quote" is ambiguous — it can mean *repair* (billable diagnostic) or *replacement* (free estimate). Clarify before you handle it:

> "Happy to get you that number — quick question so I send the right person: are you looking to fix the system you have, or are you thinking about replacing it?"

If they're replacing, there is no objection to handle: the estimate is {{price:estimate_install}} and saying so fast closes the call. See `sop.csm.service-type`. Handling a non-objection is how we talk ourselves out of a free-estimate booking.

## Why we don't quote repairs by phone {#why}

Our process is built on measurement, not guessing. Step 2 — **We Diagnose On-Site** — is what turns a symptom into a fixed-price solution:

- **iPad LiDAR scan** builds a precise 3D model of every room, ceiling height, and duct path in minutes.
- **Manual J load calculation** sizes the system to the actual home, not a rule of thumb.
- **Building-science testing** measures airflow, static pressure, duct condition, refrigerant charge, electrical, and combustion safety.
- **Thermal imaging** is used where it reveals what the eye can't see.

A phone quote skips all of this. It is a guess — and a guess we later have to revise on the doorstep costs us the job and the customer's trust. Frame the {{price:diagnostic_residential}} as the price of certainty, not a charge for showing up.

Remember what a phone quote actually does for the customer: **it lets them shop our guess against someone else's guess.** The visit is what converts. Every phone quote is a booking we chose not to make.

## What the diagnostic visit delivers {#value}

Make the fee tangible before the customer can object to it again:

1. **A 60–90 minute on-site diagnostic** by a licensed technician.
2. **Written diagnostic report** with photos and 3D scan visuals.
3. **Good / Better / Best repair or replacement options** matched to the home.
4. **Upfront, signed quote** before any work begins — no hourly surprises.
5. **Full credit of the diagnostic fee** toward same-day approved repair work.

The customer isn't paying for a look. They're paying for the answer — documented, measured, and guaranteed.

## The handling ladder {#ladder}

1. **Clarify the conversation first** (fix or replace?). Half of these calls are free-estimate bookings in disguise — don't handle an objection that isn't there.
2. **Agree with the goal.** "You want a real number — that's fair, and you'll get one: in writing, today, based on a full diagnostic." You're on their side; the visit is how they get what they asked for.
3. **Explain why a phone number hurts *them*.** A guess that doubles on the doorstep is worse than no guess. Our number is in writing before work starts — they can hold us to it.
4. **Make the visit concrete.** Name the tools — LiDAR, Manual J, static pressure, refrigerant, electrical, combustion, thermal imaging. The more specific you are, the more credible the fee becomes.
5. **Shrink the commitment.** The {{price:diagnostic_residential}} isn't the repair — it's the answer. If they don't like the repair price, they owe the fee and nothing else.
6. **Close on a window.** Don't ask *if* they want a tech — offer the window. "I can have someone there today between 2 and 4 — does that work?"

## Perfect execution — full call {#perfect}

Customer called about a furnace cycling on and off. Watch the ladder in action:

> **Customer:** "I don't want a service call, I just want a quote. How much to fix it?"
>
> **CSM:** "Happy to get you that number — quick question so I send the right person: are you looking to fix the furnace you have, or are you thinking about replacing it?"
>
> **Customer:** "Fix it. It's only six years old."
>
> **CSM:** "Makes sense — at six years it's almost always worth repairing. Here's the honest answer: I can't give you a real number over the phone, because cycling can be a $200 sensor or a $900 board, and anyone who quotes you blind is guessing. What I *can* do is get a licensed tech there today through our Home+ Protocol. The {{price:diagnostic_residential}} covers a 60–90 minute on-site diagnostic: we LiDAR-scan your home, run a Manual J load calculation, check airflow, static pressure, refrigerant, electrical, and combustion safety, then give you a written report with photos and a Good / Better / Best quote signed before any work starts. If you approve the repair, that fee is credited toward it."
>
> **Customer:** "So I pay $199 just to find out?"
>
> **CSM:** "You pay $199 for the *answer* — a written number backed by real measurements, not a guess that changes on the doorstep. If the repair price doesn't work for you, you owe the $199 and nothing else, and you keep the diagnosis."
>
> **Customer:** "…Okay. How soon can someone come?"
>
> **CSM:** "Today between 2 and 4. Can I get your address?"

**Result:** diagnostic booked at full price, no ballpark given, customer framed as protected — total handling under two minutes.

### Why each beat worked {#perfect-why}

| Beat | What the CSM did | What a weak CSM does |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Clarified fix vs. replace before handling | Assumes repair and starts defending the fee |
| Phone quote ask | Agreed with the *goal* (a real number), redirected the *method* (the diagnostic visit) | "It depends…" — dead air, customer hangs up |
| Process mention | Named specific tools: LiDAR, Manual J, static pressure, refrigerant, electrical, combustion | Vague "we have to see it first" |
| "Pay just to find out?" | Sold the answer, not the visit — written price, capped downside, credit toward work | Apologizes or hints the fee might be waived |
| Close | Offered a specific window today | "Call us back when you're ready" |

## Hard rules {#rules}

- **Never give a repair price range over the phone**, even "ballpark." A ballpark is a promise in the customer's head and a trap on the doorstep.
- **Never say "it depends" and leave it there** — replace the guess with the measured visit.
- **Never quote a fee on a replacement estimate.** If it's replacement, it's {{price:estimate_install}} — say so fast and book it.
- **The fee is the answer, not the obstacle.** Sell what the {{price:diagnostic_residential}} unlocks: LiDAR scan, Manual J, building-science testing, written report, and signed quote.
- **Name the process.** Reference "We Diagnose On-Site" or the "Home+ Protocol" on every repair-quote call. It separates us from competitors who quote blind.

## Scoring {#scoring}

QA scores this 0/1/2:

- **0** = gave a ballpark, quoted a fee on a free estimate, or failed to clarify fix vs. replace.
- **1** = held the line and explained the diagnostic value, but left the call unbooked.
- **2** = clarified type, named the on-site diagnostic process and tools, reframed the visit as the answer, and booked a window.
