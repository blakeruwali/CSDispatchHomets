# Follow-Up

> **The estimate you did not close is worth more than the next lead, and it costs nothing but a phone call. If you are not following up, you are paying to generate leads twice.**

## Sold — what happens next {#sold}

| When | Do |
|---|---|
| **Same day** | Confirmation email or text with the install date and what to expect |
| **Day 2** | Personal call: *"Just wanted to make sure you don't have any questions"* |
| **Day before install** | Confirmation with arrival time and crew info |
| **After install** | Same-day thank-you call, and ask for a Google review |
| **30 days** | Check-in: *"How's the new system treating you?"* — and the referral ask |

The referral ask, when the system has been running well for a month:

> "If you know anyone who might need heating or cooling help, I'd really appreciate the referral. We treat every customer like family."

## Unsold — the four touches {#unsold}

Most estimates that close late close on touch two or three. Log every one in ServiceTitan.

**Same day.** Leave a text before you have driven away:

> "Great meeting you today! I left the proposal on your kitchen counter. Call me anytime — [Name]"

Log the estimate with a note on **which tier they leaned toward** and what gave them pause. That note is what makes the next call useful instead of generic.

**Day 2 — a call.**

> "Hi [Name], it's [You] from Homets. Just checking in — did you have any questions about the options?"

If it goes to voicemail, leave a warm, brief message. Record their response or objection.

**Day 5 — something new to say.**

> "Hey [Name], I wanted to let you know we have install dates opening up next week. Want me to hold one for you?"

Only if that is true. If they raised a specific objection, address that one directly instead — and offer to come back and re-present if they have new questions.

**Day 10 — the last one.**

> "Hi [Name], just following up one last time. No pressure — I just want to make sure you have everything you need to make a decision."

Then stop. No response after day 10 moves to long-term nurture — a seasonal check-in, nothing more.

## Never burn the bridge {#bridge}

A customer who went with someone else may call in six months when that install goes wrong, and people remember which contractor was gracious about losing. **The last contact they have with us is the one that decides whether there is a next one.**
