# The Walk-Around

> **Thirty to forty-five minutes, photographed as you go. What you find here is the entire basis of what you recommend — and you cannot recommend anything you did not look at.**

An estimate visit is a diagnostic visit that ends differently. Everything in `sop.field.diagnostics` applies: measure it, prove it with a number, and record it. **Capture the equipment first** — every unit, plate photographed, before you start (`sop.field.equipment-capture`).

## Indoor unit {#indoor}

- Filter condition — **show it to them**
- Blower motor and capacitor
- Evaporator coil condition
- Drain pan and condensate line
- Electrical connections and voltage

## Outdoor unit {#outdoor}

- Condenser coil condition — show the buildup
- Compressor amp draw against rated
- Refrigerant level and **type — R-22 or R-410A** (this one changes the whole conversation; see `reference.diagnostic-readings`)
- Contactor and capacitor condition
- Fan motor and blade condition
- Pad, mounting, clearances

## Home and ductwork {#home}

- Thermostat type and programming
- Duct condition — visible leaks and damage
- Room-by-room temperature complaints
- Insulation visible from the attic or crawlspace
- Return air — is there enough, and is it positioned correctly?
- Electrical panel capacity for an upgrade

## Photograph everything you will later mention {#photos}

**A finding you cannot show is a finding the customer has to take on faith**, and they have no reason to. Photos of the bulging capacitor, the fouled coil, the crushed flex, the rusted pan — that is what turns a recommendation into an observation.

Narrate as you go, in front of them where you can:

> "I'm going to check your capacitor now — see this bulging? That's a sign it's failing."

Show, do not tell. The tablet does more persuading than you do.

## What you are also learning {#reading}

While you work, you are finding out which tier to lead with and whether to open with financing. A house with new windows and a finished basement is a different conversation from one where the customer flinched at the diagnostic fee. Neither judgment is about what they deserve — it is about which option you present first.

**You still present all three.** Reading the room decides emphasis, never what gets offered (`sop.sales.good-better-best`).
