# Urgency — Real and Manufactured

> **Real urgency already exists in the job. You do not have to invent any, and the moment you do, everything else you said becomes suspect.**

Most systems we look at have a genuine reason to act, and the genuine reasons are stronger than the invented ones because they survive being checked.

## Urgency that is real {#real}

Use these freely. Every one of them is verifiable by the customer.

| Trigger | How to say it |
|---|---|
| **R-22 phase-out** | "Your system uses R-22, which is no longer manufactured. Supply comes from reclaimed stock now, so every future leak repair costs more than the last." |
| **Current system risk** | "Your system is running on borrowed time. If it fails on the coldest night of the year, you're looking at an emergency call and possibly a hotel." |
| **A rebate or tax credit that genuinely exists** | "There's a [rebate] available right now for high-efficiency systems — I'd hate for you to miss it." |
| **A real seasonal backlog** | "Install dates are booking out about [X] weeks right now." |

**Two conditions on all four.** The R-22 claim must match what you actually found on the plate (`reference.diagnostic-readings`). The rebate must be one you can name and show. The backlog must be the real backlog — check, do not guess.

## Urgency that is manufactured {#manufactured}

> ⚠️ **The following were in the source sales deck and are not approved. Do not use them.**

| Tactic | Why it is out |
|---|---|
| *"This unit is in stock right now, but with supply chain issues I can't guarantee it next week."* | A shortage you have not verified. If they call back next week and it is available, you were lying. |
| *"If you move forward today I can hold your install date, otherwise it goes to the next customer."* | Not true unless it is true. We do not release held dates to punish a customer for thinking. |
| *"If we get the paperwork done today, I'll include [upgrade] at no charge."* | A discount that expires at the door is a discount that was never real, and it teaches the customer the price is arbitrary. |
| *"Interest rates are rising — locking in today protects your monthly payment."* | Speculation about the credit market, presented to a homeowner as fact. |
| *"I installed three of these in your neighborhood — your neighbor on [street] went with the Better package."* | Other customers' purchases are not ours to discuss by street. Say "several homes in this area" if it is true, and never name one. |

**Why this matters more than the ticket.** A customer who feels rushed signs, then spends the evening reading reviews, then calls to cancel — and tells people why. The tactics above trade a signature tonight for a reputation over years, on the same road where we get most of our work.

> **Owner decision required — S1.** These five come out of a deck that is live today. Confirming their removal is the owner's call and it is recorded in `_migrated/sales-reconciliation`. This document stays **in review** until then.

## What to do instead when they will not decide {#instead}

Give the decision a shape rather than a deadline:

> "I know it's a big decision. Here's what I'd suggest — let's get the financing approved, which is no obligation, so you can see the actual monthly number. Then you can decide with all the facts in front of you."

That is the one same-day technique in the source material that is entirely honest, and it is also the most effective, because most stalls are uncertainty rather than reluctance.
