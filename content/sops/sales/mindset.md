# What the Job Is

> **You are not selling a system. You are giving a family enough information to make one of the largest decisions they make about their home — and then letting them make it.**

That sentence is the standard the rest of this book is measured against. Everything here works because it is true; the moment any of it is used on someone rather than for them, it stops working and starts costing us.

## Four things this job requires {#four}

**Lead with education. The sale follows.** Present options and let them choose. Never pressure. If you find yourself trying to get past someone's judgment rather than informing it, stop — you are on the wrong side of the line and the customer can feel it before you can.

**Empathy first.** Acknowledge the frustration or the discomfort. Their budget concerns are real, not an obstacle. This is one of the biggest investments they make in their home, and they are trusting you with their family's comfort.

**Own the outcome.** Every estimate is a chance to change how a family lives in their house. Present **Good / Better / Best — always**. If you do not offer the best option, you have decided for them, and that is not yours to decide. The technician who believes in the product is the one who sells it.

**Professionalism is trust made visible.** Clean uniform, clean truck, boot covers. On time. Introduce yourself by name. Business card before you start. Treat every home like your grandmother's house.

## The line you do not cross {#the-line}

Some of what follows is technique, and technique is legitimate — a clear comparison, a monthly number, a summary at the end. What is never legitimate is manufacturing a reason to decide today.

**Anything you say to a customer has to be true when you say it.** Not defensible, not technically arguable — true. A shortage you have not confirmed, a price that is not really expiring, a neighbor's purchase you are not free to discuss. If it would embarrass you to have the customer hear the same sentence from the next three contractors, do not say it.

The company's position on this is not a preference. A signature obtained by pressure is worth less than none at all — the same principle that governs the technician and homeowner signatures on a diagnostic report (`sop.field.diagnostics`).

## Where this sits {#where}

A replacement conversation starts from a diagnosis. **The findings you present have to be the findings you measured** — `sop.field.diagnostics` is the standard for how a cause is proven, and it applies whether the visit ends in a repair or an estimate.

A replacement estimate is **{{price:estimate_install}}**. It is never a diagnostic, and the two are never quoted as if they were the same visit (`sop.csm.service-type`).
