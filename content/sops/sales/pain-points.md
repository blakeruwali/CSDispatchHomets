# Finding What They Actually Care About

> **A system is bought to solve a problem the customer already has words for. Your job is to learn their words and then use them.**

## The list {#list}

Ask, and listen for, all of these. Most homes have two or three:

| | |
|---|---|
| Uneven temperatures | Dust and allergies |
| Frequent repairs | Humidity issues |
| Noisy system | System short cycling |
| Old or outdated equipment | R-22 refrigerant |
| No cooling in certain rooms | Poor air quality |
| Safety concerns — CO, gas | Rising bills |

**Safety concerns are not a sales topic.** A CO or gas finding stops the visit and goes to `sop.field.diagnostics` §safety. It is never held back to strengthen a recommendation later — that is the single worst thing anyone in this company could do, and it would end an employment here.

## Play it back before you present {#playback}

Say their concerns out loud in their words and get agreement:

> "So it sounds like your biggest concerns are [X] and [Y] — is that fair?"

Wait for the yes. That yes is what the presentation is built on, and if you did not get it, you are about to present to a problem they do not have.

## Then tie every feature to one {#tie}

Not features. Solutions to the two things they just told you:

> "This system solves the [X] problem because…"

A variable-speed compressor means nothing to a homeowner. *"The bedroom over the garage stops being ten degrees hotter than the rest of the house"* means everything, and it is the same feature.

**A feature you cannot tie to something they said is a feature you do not mention.** It lengthens the presentation and dilutes the two things that were going to sell the job.
