# Good / Better / Best

> **Three options, every time, in writing. If you present fewer, you have made the choice for them.**

## Why all three, always {#always-three}

A customer shown one price is being told what to do. A customer shown three is being asked. The tiers are not a trick — they are how someone with no HVAC knowledge works out what they are actually buying, by seeing what changes between them.

**Presenting only what you think they can afford is the most common failure in this job**, and it is a form of deciding for someone. People routinely buy the Best option after a technician assumed they would not.

## The three tiers {#tiers}

### Good — reliable, code-compliant {#good}

- Single-stage compressor
- Basic thermostat
- Standard 1-year labor warranty
- Meets code requirements
- Most affordable upfront

> "This gets you back up and running with a reliable, code-compliant system. It's your most affordable option."

### Better — the one most people take {#better}

- Two-stage or variable speed
- Smart thermostat included
- Extended 5-year labor warranty
- Hard start kit and surge protector
- **Home+ membership included, first year**

> "This is what I'd put in my own home. You get noticeably better comfort, lower bills, and complete protection."

**Only say that sentence if it is true for you.** If you would put the Best in your own home, say that instead. Borrowed conviction is audible.

### Best — everything included {#best}

- Variable speed throughout
- Premium smart thermostat and zoning
- Lifetime compressor warranty
- UV light and air purifier included
- **Home+ membership, two years**, plus priority service

> "This is the ultimate comfort package — whisper quiet, lowest possible bills, and every add-on included. If you plan to stay 10+ years, this pays for itself."

The last clause is a real condition. **If they are selling in three years, say the Best does not pay for itself for them** — you will lose that sentence and win the customer.

## How to present them {#how}

1. **Recap the pain points.** *"Based on what we found today — [two or three issues] — here are your options."*
2. **Show the photos.** Pull them up on the tablet. Let them see the problems you described.
3. **Present Best first**, feature by feature, each tied to a problem they named.
4. **Work down.** *"If you want to save on the upfront investment, here's the Better option — still excellent, just without [X and Y]."*
5. **Pause and listen.** *"Which of these feels like the right fit for your home?"* — **then stop talking.**

Step five is the whole thing. The silence after the question belongs to the customer, and filling it is how a sale gets talked out of the room.
