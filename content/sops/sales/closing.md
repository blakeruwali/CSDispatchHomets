# Closing

> **Most customers are waiting for you to take the lead. Asking for the decision is a service, not a push — the difference is whether you accept the answer.**

Three closes, each for a different situation. Pick by what the customer is doing, not by which you like.

## The assumptive close {#assumptive}

> "Great — so let me get the paperwork started on the Better package. I can get you on the schedule for [day]."

Do not ask **if** — ask **when**.

**Use when:** they have been nodding, asking about install day, or engaging with one specific tier. They have decided and are waiting for permission.

## The either/or close {#either-or}

> "Would you like to go with the Better package, or does the Best make more sense for your family?"

Both answers are yes, and both options genuinely exist — which is what separates this from a trick.

**Use when:** they are interested but circling. It narrows the decision without removing it.

## The summary close {#summary}

> "So to recap — you're getting [system], [warranty], [membership], installed [date], for [$X/month]. Shall I lock that in?"

Restating the stack reminds them what the number buys.

**Use when:** everything has been presented and the conversation needs an ending.

## When they say yes {#yes}

> "Excellent choice. Let me get everything set up."

Then go straight to `sop.sales.paperwork` — same visit, before you leave.

## When they say no {#no}

> "I completely understand. I'll leave the proposal with you. Can I follow up [tomorrow / this week]?"

**Accept it the first time, and get a follow-up date.** A no today is very often a yes in nine days (`sop.sales.follow-up`), and the single fastest way to lose that is to ask a second time after they have already declined.

**Never ask a customer to justify a no.** *"What's there to think about?"* has never once produced a sale and reliably produces a review.
