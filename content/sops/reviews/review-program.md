# The Review Program

We opened in November 2025. Reviews are how a new company stops looking new — every booked job is a chance to turn one satisfied customer into the proof the next customer sees.

## The target {#target}

- **100+ Google reviews inside our first year of operation.**
- After year one, the count keeps climbing every month — reviews are a habit, not a campaign.

We got to our first reviews without a system. The system is how we get to a hundred and beyond: **every satisfied customer gets asked, by somebody, every time.**

## Why this matters {#why}

Good reviews increase the number of new customers day by day. For a company our age, a review is worth more than an ad — it's the difference between a caller who's already half-sold and one who's comparing us to a company with two hundred reviews.

## Everyone owns a piece {#ownership}

Reviews are not one person's side task. Each role owns the ask closest to the customer:

| Role | Their ask |
|---|---|
| **Technician** | On site, at job completion — diagnostic, repair, maintenance, or install. `sop.reviews.field-ask` |
| **CS team** | On the phone — follow-up calls and any call where the customer is delighted. `sop.reviews.cs-ask` |
| **Sales** | After a signed install or a strong sales visit that didn't close — the relationship is warmest right then |
| **Automation** | The two-day automated request after a high survey score — `sop.csm.confirmation-cadence` |

A job where nobody asked is a leak. The four channels above exist so that a missed ask in one place is caught in another — **not** so that every customer gets asked four times. One customer, one ask (plus the automation — see below).

## The hard rules — apply to everyone {#rules}

These are the same rules as `script.csm.review-request`, and they are not negotiable:

- **Never offer a discount, credit, or incentive for a review.** It violates Google's policy and can cost us the entire review profile.
- **Never say "5-star review."** Ask for honest feedback.
- **Never ask an unhappy customer.** No satisfaction expressed, no ask. A low survey score is a Manager follow-up, not a review request.
- **No pressure.** "No problem at all!" — and mean it.
- **Check before asking.** If the automation already went out, a manual ask is not a second attempt — it reads as desperate.

## Measuring it {#metrics}

Tracked monthly:

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Total Google reviews | 100+ by end of year one |
| Reviews per completed job | Rising month over month |
| Jobs closed with no review ask | Trending to zero |

The number on the profile is the scoreboard. When it stalls, the question is never "why won't customers review us" — it's "who stopped asking."
