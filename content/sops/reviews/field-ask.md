# The Technician's Review Ask

The strongest moment to ask for a review is the moment the customer is standing in front of you, happy with the work. That moment belongs to the technician — nobody else can use it.

## Every job type counts {#every-job}

The ask applies to **every completed job**, not just the big ones:

- **Diagnostics** — the customer paid {{price:diagnostic_residential}} to find out what's wrong; a clear diagnosis and an honest explanation is a reviewable experience.
- **Repairs** — the system works again. Easiest ask there is.
- **Maintenance / tune-ups** — routine, but a friendly tech on a membership visit is often the only face the customer sees all year.
- **Installs** — the highest-value job and the highest-value review. The walkthrough at the end of an install is the single best review moment we get.

## The moment {#moment}

Ask **after satisfaction is confirmed, before you leave.** Concretely: right after the customer says the work looks good, the system is running, or thanks you. Never before — never while paperwork is still open, and never cold in a follow-up text from the truck.

## The ask {#ask}

> "I'm really glad we could get this taken care of for you today. If you've got a minute, it would mean a lot to us if you could leave us a quick Google review — I can text you the link right now, it takes about 30 seconds. Would that be okay?"

Two things make it work: **"it would mean a lot to us"** (a favor between people) and **"about 30 seconds"** (removes the objection that it's a chore).

## Send the link while you're standing there {#link}

- Text the review link from the ServiceTitan app **on the spot**, so it arrives while you're still in the driveway.
- Watch them open it if they'll let you — a customer who starts the review while you're there finishes it. One who says "I'll do it later" almost never does.

## If they say no {#no}

> "No problem at all — thanks for having us out today."

Mean it. A pressured no is worse than no review — it's the one-star version of the same conversation. The CS team follow-up call is the second chance; do not burn it by pushing.

## The rules are the rules {#rules}

Same hard rules as everywhere else — no incentives, never say "5-star," never ask an unhappy customer. Full list: `sop.reviews.program`. If the visit went badly, skip the ask entirely and flag the ticket so the CS team follows up instead.
