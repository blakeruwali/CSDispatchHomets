# Second Opinion Calls

A second-opinion caller already has a diagnosis or a quote from another company and wants to know if it's right. These are some of the highest-value calls we take — the customer is already convinced something is wrong, already shopping, and one honest visit away from choosing us. Handle them with confidence, never with criticism of the other company.

## Fee posture — the one question that sets it {#fee-posture}

Everything hinges on what the other company recommended. Ask:

> "Happy to take a second look — did they quote you a **repair**, or are they recommending **replacing** the system?"

| They were quoted… | We book… | Fee |
|---|---|---|
| A replacement / new system | Install estimate (second opinion) | {{price:estimate_install}} — free |
| A repair | Repair diagnostic | {{price:diagnostic_residential}} res / {{price:diagnostic_commercial}} comm |

The replacement second opinion being free is the pitch — say it plainly:

> "There's no charge for a second opinion on a replacement quote. Our comfort advisor will look at what they proposed, check the system, and tell you straight whether you need it and what it should cost."

For a repair second opinion, the diagnostic fee applies and is framed exactly as in `sop.csm.diagnostic-fee` — and it comes off the invoice if they approve the work with us, per {{price:diagnostic_credit_on_sale}}.

## Never run down the other company {#no-bashing}

We win these calls by being more thorough and more honest, not by attacking a competitor the customer may still use.

**Never say:**
- "They ripped you off" / "that's a scam" / "they always do that"
- Any criticism of a named competitor
- A verdict on the other quote over the phone — we haven't seen the system

**Say instead:**

> "Every company looks at a system a little differently. What I can promise is a thorough look, a clear explanation of what we find, and a price in writing before any work happens."

## Booking the visit {#booking}

1. **Same standards as any booking** — full intake per `sop.csm.intake`, and nothing reaches the board without a booked ticket per `sop.csm.booking-authority`.
2. **Note it as a second opinion** on the ticket, with what the other company quoted (repair vs. replace, and the price if they'll share it). The tech walks in stronger knowing what they're being measured against.
3. **Ask them to have the other quote available** at the visit — "if you have their written quote handy, our technician can walk you through it line by line."
4. **Speed matters.** A customer holding a quote is deciding in days, not weeks. Offer the earliest honest window; these jobs justify the same urgency as a standard booking, and a same-day opening should be offered when it's real.
5. **Set the expectation:** "He'll confirm whether what they found is right, price it our way, and if their diagnosis was correct he'll tell you that too." An honest confirmation today is a replacement customer tomorrow.

## If they haven't had anyone out yet {#not-yet-diagnosed}

Sometimes "I want a second opinion" really means "I got a price over the phone" or "a friend told me it's the compressor." That's not a second opinion — it's a first diagnostic. Book it as a repair diagnostic at {{price:diagnostic_residential}} and frame the fee normally.

## Membership pivot {#membership}

A second-opinion caller who books with us hears the Home+ pitch like any other customer — see `sop.csm.membership-pivot`. A competitor's customer is often a customer whose last company never offered them anything beyond the repair.
