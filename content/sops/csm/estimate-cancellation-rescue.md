# Estimate Cancellation Rescue

A cancelled estimate is not a lost customer — it is an unfinished conversation. Every cancelled replacement estimate is roughly a five-figure opportunity walking out the door, and at our current volume this is a **$20K–$30K per month** swing. This document exists because "okay, call us back when you're ready" is the single most expensive sentence a CSM can say.

## The rule {#rule}

**Never accept a cancellation on the first ask, and never end the call without a date on the calendar or a named next action with a time attached.**

"Call us back when you're ready" is not an outcome. It is a cancellation with better manners.

## Why they really cancel {#why}

The reason they *give* is almost never the reason they cancel. Match the stated reason to what is actually happening:

| What they say | What it usually means | What actually works |
|---|---|---|
| "Just cancel it" | No perceived value in the appointment; they think we're coming to sell | Reframe the visit as a free diagnosis and a written number, no obligation |
| "I'm not home" / "Something came up" | Genuine conflict — the easiest save | Offer two specific windows, including evening/Saturday |
| "I have work / a meeting" | Appointment is inconvenient, not unwanted | Offer a window that does not need them off work; offer a spouse/tenant to let us in |
| "I'll call you back to reschedule" | Soft no — avoiding confrontation | Do not accept an open date. Book a placeholder they can move |
| "We're getting other quotes" | They will decide without our number in hand | Get on the calendar *before* the other quotes land, or we are compared to nothing |
| "We decided to hold off" | Price fear before they've seen a price | Free estimate, financing exists, no pressure — separate the *number* from the *decision* |
| Vague, no reason given | Trust gap — they don't know who's coming or why | Name the technician, the duration, and exactly what happens |

## The rescue call {#call}

Call, do not text. Within **one hour** of the cancellation, and always the same day.

**1. Open warm, no guilt.**
> "Hi [name], this is [you] with Home+ Air and Heat — I saw your estimate for [day] came off the schedule. Totally fine, I just didn't want to leave you without a plan. What happened on your end?"

**2. Listen and let them finish.** The real reason usually comes out in the second sentence, not the first.

**3. Reframe the visit — this is the step that saves the job.**
> "Just so you know what you're turning down — this isn't a sales call. [Tech] comes out, tests the system, and leaves you a written number with three options. It's free, there's no obligation, and you'd have it in writing whether you do anything this year or not."

**4. Remove the reason.** Address the specific barrier from the table above. One barrier, one answer.

**5. Assume the reschedule with two choices, never an open question.**
> "I've got Thursday between 2 and 4, or Saturday morning. Which is easier?"

Never: "When would you like to reschedule?" That question hands them the cancellation back.

## When they still say "just cancel it" {#hard-no}

Two attempts, then stop. Pushing a third time costs us the review, not just the job.

**Attempt one — the value ask:**
> "Understood. Can I ask — is it the timing, or has something changed about the system itself?"

**Attempt two — the placeholder close:**
> "Here's what I'd suggest so you're not stuck if it fails on a hot day: let me hold you a spot for [date]. Nothing is owed, no one shows up if you cancel, and you can move it with one text. That way you have a slot instead of a three-day wait in August."

A held placeholder converts far better than an open "call us back," because it makes *doing nothing* the action that requires a decision.

**If they still decline:** accept it graciously, record the real reason, and move them to nurture — never argue, never re-pitch.
> "No problem at all. I'll text you my direct line so you have a person, not a phone tree. If anything changes — even just a question — text me."

## The 30-day nurture (never let it die at the cancel) {#nurture}

Every declined estimate enters this sequence unless the customer asks not to be contacted:

| When | Touch | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Same day | Text | Direct line + "no obligation, here if you need us" |
| Day 3 | Text | One useful thing about *their* system — age, symptom, what to watch for |
| Day 10 | Call | "Did you end up getting it looked at?" — genuinely ask, don't pitch |
| Day 30 | Text | Seasonal or promotional angle, if one exists |
| Season change | Call | First heat wave / first cold snap — highest-converting touch we have |

The first hot or cold day after a cancellation is when a dead estimate comes back to life. Someone has to make that call.

## Prevention beats rescue {#prevention}

Most cancellations are set up at booking, not at cancellation. Fix them upstream:

- **Set expectations at booking** — say the visit is free, roughly an hour, and produces a written number with options. A customer who knows what's coming does not cancel out of confusion.
- **Name the technician** at confirmation. A name shows up; a company does not.
- **Confirm the day before**, not the morning of. Same-day confirmations catch the cancellation too late to refill the slot.
- **Ask who needs to be there.** If a spouse has to see the number, book a time both can attend — that alone kills a large share of "let me talk to my wife" cancellations later. See `sop.csm.reschedules`.
- **Book decision-makers into afternoon or Saturday windows** when they mention work.

## Never do this {#never}

- Never say "no problem, just call us back when you're ready" — it is a cancellation with a smile on it.
- Never discount to save an estimate. The estimate is already free; discounting before a diagnosis destroys the technician's pricing conversation. See `sop.csm.reschedules`.
- Never guilt or pressure. Two attempts, then nurture.
- Never close the file without a recorded reason — the reason is how we fix the booking process.
- Never let a cancellation sit until tomorrow. Rescue rates fall off a cliff after the same day.

## What gets measured {#measure}

Reported weekly to the service manager alongside booking rate:

- **Estimate cancellation rate** — cancels ÷ estimates booked
- **Rescue rate** — cancelled estimates put back on the calendar (target: **50%+**)
- **Nurture conversion** — declined estimates that book within 90 days
- **Reason breakdown** — the top cancellation reason each month gets one fix in the booking process

Three cancellations from the same lead source or the same booking CSM in a week is a process problem, not a customer problem — bring it to the one-to-one.
