# Difficult Customers & De-escalation

## The four moves {#moves}

1. **Lower your volume.** Match their pace, not their heat.
2. **Acknowledge the feeling.** "I hear you, and I'd feel the same way."
3. **Take ownership of the next step, not the past problem.** "Here's what I'm going to do right now."
4. **Never argue facts mid-heat.** Get the callback booked or escalate.

## Why "next step, not past problem" {#next-step}

An angry customer is not asking you to relitigate what happened. Defending the company mid-heat reads as dismissal and doubles the temperature. You do not have to agree that we were wrong to say what happens next — and what happens next is the only thing that calms anyone down.

Owning the past problem is the Manager's call, not the CSM's. Owning the next step is always yours.

## Escalate to Manager immediately {#escalate}

Stop de-escalating and hand off the moment any of these appear:

- Customer asks for a manager.
- Customer threatens a review, the BBB, or an attorney.
- Any discrimination or harassment claim.
- Repeat caller with a prior complaint on the account.

These are not failures of your handling — they are the defined boundary of the role. See `sop.csm.role`.

Hand off warm, never blind: brief the Manager on the customer's name, the issue, and what you've already done. Procedure in `sop.csm.transfer-hold`.

## What a CSM never does {#never}

- Never argue.
- Never waive a fee to end a hard call — fee waivers are Manager-only. See `sop.csm.diagnostic-fee`.
- Never hang up first.
- Never close the file on a cancellation that came with a complaint. See `sop.csm.reschedules`.
