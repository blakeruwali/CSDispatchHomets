# Arrival & Conduct in the Home

> **Shoe covers go on before you step inside. Every visit, every home, without being asked.**

`sop.field.start-time` gets you to the door at the top of the window. This document is everything from the door inward.

## The 60-second version {#field-card}

1. **Park** so you are not blocking them in.
2. **Open the job** — symptom, history, membership status.
3. **Knock, step back**, introduce yourself by name and company.
4. **Shoe covers on** before the first step inside.
5. **Ask** about pets, children, alarm, and where the equipment is.
6. **Drop cloth down** before tools come out.
7. **Leave it cleaner than you found it**, and walk the space with them before you go.

## Why this is in the SOP at all {#why}

Customers cannot judge a capacitor. They have no way to know whether you diagnosed correctly or guessed, and they will never find out. So they judge us on what they *can* assess — whether you looked them in the eye, whether the floor was clean when you left, whether you seemed like someone they would let in again.

That is not unfair. It is the only evidence they have.

Read the one-star reviews of any HVAC company on Long Island. Almost none of them say "the repair failed." They say *tracked mud through my house*, *left the old parts in my yard*, *never told me his name*. The work was probably fine. Nobody remembers.

So the ninety seconds this document describes decides the review, the membership conversation, and whether they call us or the next company in January. It is worth more per hour than anything else you do on the job.

## Before you knock {#before-knock}

**Park considerately.** Not across the driveway, not blocking a car that may need to leave, never on the lawn. The street or the curb is the default. In a tight driveway, leave them a way out — a customer who is boxed in for two hours is annoyed before you have said a word.

**Open the job in ServiceTitan.** Symptom, address notes, prior visits, and membership status — see `sop.field.membership`, which is checked *before* the knock so you can acknowledge it at the door.

**Look at what you are about to walk into.** Newly finished floors, a wheelchair ramp, kids' toys on the path, a *beware of dog* sign. Ten seconds in the driveway tells you most of what you need.

**Check yourself.** Clean uniform, shirt tucked, name visible. You are about to ask a stranger to let you into their home.

## At the door {#at-the-door}

**Knock or ring once, then step back from the door.** Standing right against it is intimidating, particularly to someone answering alone. A pace back changes how the whole visit opens.

**Introduce yourself before anything else:**

> "Good morning — I'm [name] with Homets, here for your [no-heat call]. Alright if I come take a look?"

Name, company, reason, permission. Four things, six seconds.

**If nobody answers**, knock again, call the number on the job, and then call Dispatch. Do not leave until Dispatch tells you to — and never go around the back of a house uninvited.

## Shoe covers {#shoe-covers}

**Covers go on at the threshold, on every visit, before your first step inside.** Not after the customer looks at your boots. Not only when the floor looks nice. Every time.

- **Carry enough.** A box lives on the truck and gets restocked like any other consumable. "I ran out" is a stocking failure, the same as arriving without a capacitor.
- **Fresh pair when they tear or soak through.** A torn cover is worse than none — it drags whatever you picked up across the floor.
- **Re-cover every time you come back in.** Out to the truck for a part, back in, new covers or the same clean ones — but never straight in from the driveway.
- **Do it where they can see you.** Half the value is the customer watching you do it unprompted.

**The one exception is safety.** Covers on a ladder, on open stairs, or on a wet basement floor are a slip hazard, and a fall costs more than a footprint. Take them off for the ladder or the unfinished space, then put fresh ones on before you step back onto finished floor. This exception is about footing and nothing else — never convenience, never "it's only the hallway."

**If the customer tells you not to bother**, you may take them off. It is their home. But they only get to make that call because you already had them on — and wipe your boots on the mat anyway.

> "I'll get these on before I come in."

Say it while you are already doing it.

## What to ask, before you start {#ask-first}

- **"Any pets I should know about?"** — a door left open by a technician is how a cat gets lost, and that call never ends well.
- **"Anyone home I should be careful not to wake?"** — night-shift workers, a sleeping baby.
- **"Is there an alarm I'll set off getting to the unit?"**
- **"Can you show me where it is?"** — and let them walk you there. That walk is where they tell you the thing that was not in the ticket.
- **"Tell me what it's been doing."** In their words, before your diagnosis. Same principle as `sop.field.diagnostics`: the customer's description is data.

## Protecting the work area {#protect}

**Drop cloth down before tools come out.** Under the unit, and anywhere you will be setting parts or standing repeatedly.

- Tools and parts go on the cloth — never on carpet, never on furniture, never on the water heater.
- **Nothing gets leaned against a wall** that will mark it.
- **The old part and its packaging leave with you.** A used capacitor left on a basement shelf is a complaint waiting six months to happen.
- **Contain the mess where it starts.** A filter change over a drop cloth is thirty seconds; the same change over cream carpet can cost an afternoon.

This is the same standard `sop.projects.install-day` holds install crews to. A service call is not a lesser visit.

## While you are in the home {#conduct}

- **No smoking or vaping**, on the property at all — including in the truck in their driveway.
- **No music or speakerphone.** Personal calls happen outside, and briefly.
- **Ask before you use anything** — a bathroom, an outlet, a hose, a ladder. Their ladder is never worth the liability; use your own.
- **Ask before moving their belongings**, and put them back exactly as they were.
- **Narrate what you are doing** when they are watching. Silence reads as something going wrong.
- **Never criticize the previous technician or installer**, even when they deserve it. It sounds like a sales tactic and it makes the customer feel foolish for their last decision. Describe the condition, not the person who caused it.
- **Mind what you say on the phone in earshot.** A muttered comment to Dispatch in a quiet house is audible.

## Before you leave {#before-leaving}

- ☐ Old parts, packaging, and the filter wrapper are **on the truck**, not in their bin
- ☐ Work area vacuumed or wiped — you brought a shop vac, use it
- ☐ Panels and covers back on, screws in, thermostat set back to where they had it
- ☐ Anything you moved is back where it was
- ☐ Drop cloth up, shoe covers off **at the threshold**, not two rooms early
- ☐ Walk the space with them and confirm out loud that it is as you found it

> "Before I go — mind walking through with me so you can see it's all back the way it was?"

Thirty seconds. It is also the natural opening for the review ask in `sop.reviews.field-ask`, and a customer who has just agreed the space is clean is a customer in the right frame of mind to be asked.

## Worked example {#example}

**Weak:** Tech pulls into the driveway behind the customer's car. Knocks, stands close to the door, opens with "you the no-heat?" Walks in in work boots, sets a toolbag on the carpet, changes a capacitor in eleven minutes, leaves the old one on the furnace and a filter wrapper on the floor. Repair is correct and the system runs for four more years. The review says *fixed it fast but tracked snow through my hallway and left trash in my basement.* Three stars, and it stays up forever.

**Perfect:** Same repair. Parks on the street, opens the job in the driveway and sees an active Home+ membership, knocks and steps back, introduces himself by name, covers on at the threshold, asks about the dog, drop cloth down, capacitor changed, old part and wrapper to the truck, area wiped, walks the basement with the customer, mentions the membership discount already applied. Twelve extra minutes. Five stars naming him personally, and the tune-up renews in spring.

| | Weak | Perfect |
|---|---|---|
| Parking | Blocked the customer in | Street, driveway clear |
| Introduction | "You the no-heat?" | Name, company, reason, permission |
| Footwear | Boots, snow in the hallway | Covers on before the first step |
| Old part | Left on the furnace | On the truck |
| Extra time | 0 minutes | ~12 minutes |
| Review | 3 stars, cleanliness complaint | 5 stars, names the tech |

## When it goes wrong {#failures}

- **You ran out of covers.** Do not walk in anyway. Boots off at the door if that is workable, or call Dispatch — and restock that night. Recovery is possible; pretending the rule did not apply is not.
- **Pouring rain, deep mud, or snow.** Boots off outside *and* covers on, and consider a second cloth by the door. Bad weather is when this matters most, not when it is excused.
- **Crawlspace, attic, or an unfinished basement.** Covers off for footing, and then a **fresh pair** before you re-enter finished space. Crawlspace insulation on a living room floor is the exact failure this rule exists to prevent.
- **Purely outdoor job — condenser only.** Covers still go on the moment you step inside for the thermostat or the disconnect, even for thirty seconds.
- **The customer is following you and asking questions while you work.** Answer them. An interested customer is a customer who is about to buy something. If you genuinely need quiet for a reading, say so plainly: *"Give me two minutes on this reading and I'll walk you through what I'm seeing."*
- **Something gets damaged.** Tell the customer immediately, photograph it, and call Dispatch before you leave the property. A reported accident is an insurance matter; an unreported one that they find later is a dispute we lose on credibility alone.
- **A tenant answers, not the owner.** Do the work, but anything touching money or replacement goes through the owner — `sop.csm.customer-profiles`.

### Hard rules

- Never step onto a finished floor in work boots. If covers are genuinely off for footing, they go back on at the edge of finished space.
- Never leave an old part, filter, or packaging at the customer's home.
- Never smoke or vape on a customer's property, including in the truck.
- Never go into an area of the home you were not invited into.
- Never leave without walking the space and confirming it is as you found it.
- Never criticize the previous technician.

## QA scoring {#qa}

| Score | Standard |
|---|---|
| 2 | Parked considerately, introduced by name, covers on unprompted at the threshold, pets and access asked about, drop cloth used, all debris removed, space walked with the customer before leaving |
| 1 | Covers worn and area left clean, but one element missed — no introduction by name, no closing walkthrough, or an old part left behind |
| 0 | Entered in work boots, or left debris, marks, or a mess the customer had to deal with after we drove away |
