# Equipment & Material Procurement

| | |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | The right equipment is on site the day before the crew is, every time. |
| **When it applies** | Every project, immediately after handoff verification. |
| **Owner** | Project Manager. |

## Order within one business day {#timing}

Handoff verified → order placed. Equipment lead time drives the install date, so nothing else about the schedule can be promised until the order is confirmed.

## What "ordered" means {#confirmed-order}

An order is not placed until you have, written on the job:

- Supplier name and order/PO number
- Each line item with model number and quantity
- **Confirmed delivery or will-call date**
- Where it lands: warehouse, supplier hold, or direct to site

A verbal "we should have that" from a counter rep is not an order. Get the confirmation in writing.

## The materials list is part of the order {#materials}

Equipment gets remembered; materials cause the second trip. Order with the unit: line set, whip and disconnect, pad or stand, condensate pump and drain, venting, plenum and transition metal, thermostat, filter cabinet, refrigerant, and anything the photos told you this specific house needs.

## Backorders {#backorders}

The moment a lead time slips past the promised install date:

1. Tell the Project Manager's own customer the same day — before they find out by waiting.
2. Offer the alternative honestly: a comparable model at the same or better spec at **no price increase to the customer**, or the original unit on the later date. Their choice.
3. If the house is without heat or cooling, arrange temporary equipment and treat it as an emergency install — see `sop.dispatch.priorities`.

We never let a customer sit past a promised date in silence. The delay is forgivable; the silence is not.

## Staging {#staging}

Equipment is staged and physically verified **the day before install** — right model, right quantity, undamaged, all accessories present. A crate opened for the first time at 8 AM on install day is a lost day.
