# Sales-to-Project Handoff

| | |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | The project starts from verified facts, not from what someone remembers being said in the kitchen. |
| **When it applies** | Within one business day of any sold install. |
| **Owner** | Selling technician submits; Project Manager verifies. |

## What the selling technician submits {#submit}

Before end of day on the day it is sold:

- Signed agreement, all pages, in the job.
- Deposit collected and posted.
- **Photos**: existing equipment data plates, the mechanical space from three angles, the electrical panel, the line set / venting route, and the path the equipment has to travel to get in.
- Equipment selected, by model number — not "the mid-range 3-ton".
- Anything promised verbally: a thermostat, a disposal, haul-away, a filter cabinet, "we'll fix that duct while we're in there". If it is not written down it will not be installed, and the customer will remember it anyway.

## What the Project Manager verifies {#verify}

Do not order anything before all seven clear:

1. **Scope matches the photos.** A 3-ton on a 2.5-ton pad with a 40-year-old line set is a conversation, not an order.
2. **Model numbers exist and are available.** Confirm against the supplier, not the catalogue.
3. **Address is the service address**, not the billing address.
4. **Two contacts** — the decision maker and whoever will actually be home.
5. **Deposit posted**, at the amount the agreement states.
6. **Access noted** — stairs, crawl space, gate codes, dogs, parking, elevator reservations for commercial.
7. **Permit likely required?** Flag it now — `sop.projects.permits`.

## When something does not clear {#exceptions}

Call the selling technician the same day, not the customer. The technician owns their own scope correction. If the correction changes the price, the technician re-presents it to the customer and a revised agreement is signed **before** anything is ordered.

## The customer hears from us within 24 hours {#customer-touch}

Whoever verifies, calls: "This is [name], I'm the project manager on your new system. I've got your file, I'm ordering your equipment today, and I'll call you with an install date by [day]." That one call is the difference between a confident customer and a customer shopping for a second quote while they wait.
