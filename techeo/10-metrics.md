# Techeo's Seven Numbers & Review Cadence

**The same discipline Homets applies to its CSM desk, applied to Techeo.** `content/sops/csm/kpis.md` works because it is seven numbers, all inside one role's control, reviewed weekly against a written target. This is that, for a marketing company.

## The numbers {#numbers}

| # | Metric | Target | Why it is here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Founder hours / week on Techeo** | ≤ 26 | The binding constraint of the entire business. Listed first on purpose. |
| 2 | **Homets KPI compliance** | Pass | The kill switch from `07`. A fail overrides every other number on this page. |
| 3 | **Median client booking-rate lift** | ≥ +10 pts by day 90 | The product metric. If this is not moving, nothing else is real. |
| 4 | **Audit → retainer conversion** | ≥ 50% | Below 35% is a qualification problem, not an audit problem. |
| 5 | **MRR** | Per the Base scenario in `06` | The scoreboard. Deliberately fourth, not first. |
| 6 | **Margin after imputed founder time** | ≥ 65% | Catches the "90% margin, 70-hour week" illusion before it sets in. |
| 7 | **Logo churn** | 0 in the first 12 months | With three clients, one loss is a third of the company and the end of the referral engine. |

Metrics 1 and 2 sit above revenue because of the arithmetic in `07`: a 5% slip at Homets costs roughly four times what a good first year of Techeo creates. An MRR number read without those two beside it is misleading.

## How they are read {#reading}

- **Founder hours** are logged weekly as they happen, not reconstructed monthly. A reconstructed number is always too low.
- **Homets KPI compliance** is a pass/fail carried over from the CSM weekly review — not re-derived here, and not reported by the founder.
- **Booking-rate lift** is measured against the week-0 baseline the client signed off on. This is why the signature in `04-delivery.md` matters.
- **Conversion** counts audits delivered, not audits sold. An audit paid for but never read did not convert and should not flatter the number.
- **Margin** imputes founder time at $100/hr. The imputation is the whole point; without it the metric always looks fine.
- **Churn** is counted at notice given, not at the final invoice.

## Cadence {#cadence}

| When | What |
|---|---|
| **Weekly** (30 min, Friday) | Hours logged. Pipeline moved. Client scorecards out. One line on what did not get done. |
| **Monthly** (by the 10th) | Books closed. All seven numbers published in the same document as the Homets KPIs. |
| **Quarterly** | Engine re-scores for every client. Price review. Re-read `07` out loud. Update these documents. |

The monthly publication is a single document containing both companies' numbers. Separate reports let one business hide behind the other; one page makes the trade-off visible every month, which is the only way it stays a decision rather than a drift.

## Client-facing reporting {#client-reporting}

One page. One headline number: **cost per booked job**, trended against baseline. Four engine scores beneath it. Three lines: what we did, what it moved, what is next.

Never longer in year one. The industry's reports are bloated because length is where the absence of a number that matters gets hidden.

## What is not measured {#not-measured}

No impressions, no rankings-as-an-outcome, no "engagement," no lead volume without the booking rate beside it, no hours billed. Each of these can improve while the client's business gets worse, which makes them worse than useless — they are how an agency argues it is winning while its client is losing.
