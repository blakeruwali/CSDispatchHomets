# Delivery — What a Client Actually Receives

**The productization rule: every client engagement must leave behind a reusable template, or it was delivered wrong.** Bespoke work that vanishes into one client's account is how an agency stays a job instead of becoming a company.

## Booking Sprint — the 90 days {#sprint}

| Week | Work | Client-visible deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Kickoff call. Access confirmed. Baseline frozen and signed off by the owner. | Baseline report — the numbers we will be judged against |
| 1 | Fork the Homets CSM SOP set. Replace every price and policy with theirs. | Draft SOP set for their review |
| 2 | Map their lead sources to channel playbooks. Set SLAs. Instrument in ServiceTitan. | Playbook set + SLA sheet |
| 3 | Live training session 1 — greeting, qualification, booking, the diagnostic fee | Recorded training, acknowledgement sheet |
| 4 | Call scoring begins. 5 calls scored. First coaching note to the CS lead. | Week 1 scorecard |
| 5–8 | Weekly: 5 scored calls, coaching note, KPI report. Speed-to-lead instrumentation completed and verified. | Weekly scorecard + KPI report |
| 9 | Live training session 2 — objections, membership pitch, marketplace outreach | Recorded training |
| 10–11 | Weekly scoring continues. Dashboard build. Gaps from the audit re-measured. | Weekly scorecard; dashboard access |
| 12 | 90-day review: baseline vs current, engine re-score, next-quarter plan | Review deck + Demand Engine proposal |

**Week 0 baseline sign-off is the most important hour of the engagement.** An owner who has not agreed in writing what "before" looked like will remember it differently at week 12. Get a signature on the baseline report.

## Demand Engine — the monthly rhythm {#rhythm}

| Engine | Cadence | Work |
|---|---|---|
| Booking | Weekly | 5 scored calls, coaching note, SLA compliance check |
| Ads | Weekly | Search-term negatives, budget pacing vs seasonality, LSA lead rating and feedback, bid adjustments |
| Search | Monthly | 2 service-area pages, GBP posts, review-velocity operations, technical fix queue |
| Answer | Monthly | Entity and structured-data work, the 12-prompt citation re-test, content written to be quoted |
| Reporting | Monthly | One page. Headline number: **cost per booked job.** |
| Strategy | Quarterly | Engine re-score, plan, and the price conversation if scope has grown |

**LSA lead rating is quietly one of the highest-ROI recurring tasks we perform.** Google ended manual lead disputes in 2024: an automated review now credits leads it judges invalid, and rating a bad lead in the Leads tab is the signal that triggers a second look. Most contractors never rate their leads, so they never recover credits they are owed. It is mechanical enough to delegate early. Credits remain Google's decision — never promise one. *(Platform behavior changes; re-verify against Google's Local Services help pages each quarter.)*

## The reporting standard {#reporting}

One page. One headline number. Cost per booked job, trended against the baseline, with the four engine scores beneath it and a three-line "what we did / what it moved / what's next."

Never send a report longer than a page in year one. The industry's reporting is bloated precisely because it is hiding the absence of a number that matters. A single honest number, monthly, is a competitive weapon.

## Templated vs bespoke {#templating}

| Always templated | Always bespoke |
|---|---|
| SOP set (fork, then find-and-replace their prices) | Their price and policy values |
| Channel playbooks | Which channels they actually run |
| QA rubric and scorecard | Individual coaching notes |
| Mystery-shop scenarios | Nothing |
| Monthly report layout | The numbers in it |
| Service-area page structure | Town names, local proof, photos |
| The 12 AEO prompts | City substitution |

Anything in the left column that gets rebuilt per client is a delivery defect. Fix the template, not the instance.

## Tooling stack {#stack}

Start deliberately thin. Every tool added is a fixed cost that must be carried in the lean months.

| Need | Tool | Approx cost |
|---|---|---|
| Rank tracking | Local rank tracker | $50–100/mo |
| Call tracking / recording | Call tracking platform | $50–150/mo per client, billed to client |
| Reporting | Looker Studio | Free |
| Project management | ClickUp (already in use) | existing |
| Docs / SOP hosting | This repo pattern, reused | ~free |
| Site audits | Screaming Frog | ~$260/yr |
| Client comms | Email + Slack Connect | existing |
| Contracts / e-sign | Any e-sign tool | $15–30/mo |
| Bookkeeping | Accounting software + bookkeeper | $200–400/mo |

Deliberately **not** buying in year one: an all-in-one agency platform, a white-label SEO vendor, an AI content tool subscription, or a CRM. The first three clients can be run on the list above.

## Capacity — the real ceiling {#capacity}

| Engagement | Founder hours / client / month |
|---|---|
| Booking Sprint (months 1–3) | 14–18 |
| Booking Sprint (steady state) | 8–10 |
| Demand Engine | 16–20 |
| Audit (one-off) | 8 |

At 20 hours per Demand Engine client, **five clients is 100 hours a month** before any selling, admin, or Homets. That is the arithmetic behind the gate in `07-homets-guardrails.md`: the ceiling is not ambition, it is hours, and it arrives at client four or five.

The first delegation target is call scoring — it is rubric-driven, trainable, and roughly 40% of recurring hours. A part-time scorer at $20–25/hr buys back the most time per dollar of anything on this list.

## Offboarding {#offboarding}

When a client leaves, they get everything: their SOP set as editable documents, their dashboards, their playbooks, full account ownership confirmed in writing, and a 30-minute handover call. No hostage-taking, no data withheld, no exceptions.

This costs nothing and is worth a great deal. The contractor world is small and departing clients are the most credible referral source we will ever have.
