# Go To Market — Channels, Design Partners, Scripts

**We are not an agency trying to reach contractors. We are a contractor talking to peers. That is the whole channel strategy and it should be visible in every touch.**

## Channels, ranked {#channels}

Ranked by expected return per hour in the first year, not by reach.

| # | Channel | Why it works here | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Peer networks** — ServiceTitan Pantheon, Nexstar, Service Nation / Roundtable, EGIA | Rooms full of exactly our ICP who already vet vendors by peer reference. Membership buys standing. | Medium, high conviction |
| 2 | **Adjacent vendor referrals** — ST implementation consultants, trades bookkeepers, recruiters, financing reps | They already know who is growing, who is disorganised, and who just lost their marketing person. Highest-intent introductions available. | Low |
| 3 | **Owner communities** — HVAC/plumbing owner Facebook groups, trades podcasts | Where owners complain about their agencies in public. Answer the complaint, do not pitch. | Low, slow compounding |
| 4 | **Supply house relationships** — Ferguson, Johnstone counters, manufacturer reps | Counter conversations reach owners nothing else does. Co-marketing with a rep is warm by default. | Low |
| 5 | **Own content** — the operator angle | "The HVAC owner who runs his own marketing" is a genuinely differentiated byline. Publish the Homets numbers. | High, slowest |
| 6 | **Trade shows** — AHR Expo, regional | Expensive; only worth it with three case studies in hand. Year two. | High cost |
| 7 | **Cold outbound** | Only ever with a teardown attached. Never a generic email. | Medium, lowest conviction |

Pick **two** for the first six months. Recommended: peer networks (#1) and adjacent vendor referrals (#2). #2 costs almost nothing and pays fastest; #1 is where the retainers are. Everything else waits.

## The teardown-first motion {#teardown}

The only outbound we do. Shop their phone, score it, send the evidence.

1. Call their published number as a homeowner, using scenario 1 or 3 from `03-engine-audit.md`
2. Score against the rubric
3. Submit their web form and time the callback
4. Record a 15-minute screen walkthrough of what happened
5. Send it unrequested, with no pitch and one question

**Consent warning, read before the first call.** Recording law varies by state — some require all parties to consent. In a two-party-consent state, do not record; deliver structured notes against the rubric instead, which works nearly as well. Confirm the rule for each target's state before dialling, and see `08-legal-and-admin.md`.

## Design Partner Programme {#design-partners}

Three slots. This is how we buy case studies, and it must be a written agreement, not a favour between contractors.

**We give:** 50% off the Booking Sprint — $1,750/mo for six months, then standard pricing.

**They give:**

1. Baseline data access and monthly data thereafter
2. A named case study with real numbers, approved for publication
3. A recorded video testimonial at day 90
4. Two introductions to non-competing peers
5. A 30-minute feedback call monthly
6. Permission to use their Engine Score movement in sales material

**Selection criteria — all six, no exceptions:**

- Outside Homets' market and outside each other's markets
- ServiceTitan user
- Existing lead flow to recover
- Owner is the decision maker and decides quickly
- Willing to be named publicly
- Will commit their CS staff to training time

Take the slower, more organised contractor over the faster, chaotic one. A design partner who does not implement produces no case study, and a failed first reference is worse than no reference.

**Stop at three.** The fourth discounted client is not a case study, it is underpriced revenue.

## Market exclusivity {#exclusivity}

**One client per market per trade, granted in writing in the MSA.**

Three jobs at once:

1. **It resolves the conflict of interest.** We own an HVAC company; a prospect's first thought is competition. Exclusivity plus the Homets exclusion zone in `07` answers it before it is asked.
2. **It is the close.** "There is one HVAC slot in your metro and it is open right now" is an honest scarcity, unlike most scarcity in this industry.
3. **It protects delivery.** We cannot run the same local pack strategy for two competitors, and an agency that tries is defrauding one of them.

Define "market" precisely in the contract — DMA, or a named county list — because a vague grant becomes a dispute the first time a nearby contractor calls.

## Scripts {#scripts}

### Warm peer-network introduction

> Hey {name} — Blake, I own Homets Air and Heat down in {market}. We spent the last year building out our CSM system: written SOPs, SLAs on every lead source, weekly call scoring. Our qualified booking rate target is 70% and the marketplace leads don't sit anymore.
>
> Enough people have asked how we did it that I've started doing it for a few shops outside my market. Not pitching you — I wanted to ask whether {network} would be a reasonable place to share the actual numbers, and whether that's useful to anyone there.

### Teardown cold outreach

> Subject: I called your office Tuesday morning — 4 minutes, here's what happened
>
> {Name} — I own an HVAC company in {market}, so this is peer to peer rather than a sales email.
>
> I called your office Tuesday at 9:30 as a homeowner with no cooling upstairs, and submitted your web form the same morning. I scored both against the standard we hold our own CSMs to. Here it is — 14 minutes, no pitch: {link}
>
> The short version: {one specific, factual, non-insulting observation}.
>
> If it's useful, keep it. If you want the same thing done properly across all your lead sources, reply and I'll explain what that looks like. Either way the recording is yours.

Never open with flattery, never use the word "audit" in outbound, and never lead with a compliment about their website. One specific observation beats any amount of framing.

### Adjacent vendor referral ask

> {Name} — you see inside more {trade} shops in a month than I do in a year. I've started doing marketing and conversion work for HVAC and plumbing companies outside my own market, and the ones I can help most are $2–10M, on ServiceTitan, already buying leads and losing a chunk of them on the phone.
>
> If anyone comes to mind, I'd rather you send them a free teardown of their phone handling than an introduction to me — it's more useful to them and it makes you look good. Happy to produce those on request, no strings.

Giving the vendor something to *give* rather than asking for a favour is the whole trick.

## Sales process {#process}

| Stage | Advances when | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Identified | Meets ICP, outside exclusion zones | — |
| Teardown sent | Evidence delivered | 30% reply |
| Discovery call | 30 min, their numbers, their pain | 50% of replies |
| Audit sold | $1,500 paid | 40% of calls |
| Audit delivered | Readout call completed | 100% |
| Retainer signed | MSA + SOW executed | ≥50% of audits |

Expected cycle: **6–10 weeks** from first touch to signed retainer. Budget for it. Seasonality makes it worse — see the calendar in `07-homets-guardrails.md`, and never plan to close business in July or late December.

## What we do not do {#no}

No paid ads to acquire clients in year one — we would be competing on cost per lead with agencies who do it full time, using the hours we do not have. No cold calling. No lead-gen list purchases. No pay-per-lead marketplaces. No partnerships that require giving up account ownership.
